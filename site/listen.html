Mushroom Substrate

Listen

Grow a network, record it, and hear it as a physical model: bodies that can vibrate, set moving by the forces the simulation says acted on them. No sound is assigned to anything. No fungus has been recorded making these sounds, and real hyphae are too damped to ring at all.

What vibrates

The network
A web of taut strings. A hypha is a tube held taut by its own turgor pressure, which gives a wave speed of about 22 metres per second whatever its width. Thick cords are heavier and tighter, so waves partly reflect where thick meets thin. Its notes are worked out from its real size, shape and cord thickness, eight or nine times as it grows, and they fall as it gets bigger.
The mushrooms
Each cap is a small plate on a stem. A soft cap 3 cm in radius has a lowest note near 90 Hz, and the note rises as the cap gets smaller, so a cap's note falls while it expands.

What sets them moving

Growth
On the network: a branch breaking out, two walls dissolving where hyphae fuse, a piece losing its pressure when it is abandoned or killed. Each is one hypha's turgor force, about ten millionths of a newton, acting for a millisecond, at the place it happened. On a cap: the same small yields at random, for as long as the cap is expanding.
Electrical spikes
Each spike at an electrode is taken as a brief 1% change in turgor there. Nobody has measured such a pressure pulse; this is an assumption, and the spike recordings themselves are disputed.
Spores leaving
A mature cap sheds about 30,000 spores a second at about a metre per second. Each launch pushes back on the cap. Only the stand-scale engine sheds spores.
Rain
Drops 4 mm across at 9 metres per second, 5 mm of rain in an hour. Drops strike caps directly, and drops on the ground reach the hyphae beneath, weakened with depth.

What is real and what is not

Every strike rings at its real pitch for its real length; only the schedule is compressed, by the one time-lapse factor shown under the player. The forces differ enormously (a raindrop against a spore), so each is brought to an audible level separately, and the text under the player says how much quieter each really is in the model. Two microphones sit at the surface, a fifth of the way in from each side.

What is not physical: the ring. Real hyphae and caps are damped almost at once, so the honest ring time is near zero and the slider is a choice. The strings have no soil around them, the caps no gills or flesh, and several numbers (how long a yield lasts, how many yields a growing cap makes, the pressure of a spike) are assumptions.
