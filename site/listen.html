Mushroom Substrate

Listen

Grow a network, record it, and hear it. No fungus has been recorded making these sounds: mycelium is silent as far as anyone has measured. What you hear is the model's slow rhythms sped up into the range of hearing, by amounts stated under the player.

What each sound is

Electrode voices
Four electrodes sit on the network. Each one's voltage is played directly as a sound wave, spread from left to right. The engine's electrical rhythm takes minutes to hours per cycle; it is sped up until one cycle lands at the pitch you choose. Voices drift in and out of step as the network grows and its tips move.
Growth events
A tick when a hypha branches, a knock when two fuse, a soft hiss when one is abandoned, a tone when a mushroom matures, a growl where rivals meet, a wash of noise for rain. Left to right is where it happened; higher is shallower, lower is deeper.
Network resonance
The network treated as a web of taut strings. Each growth event strikes the web where it happened, and the four electrodes act as pickups. The notes are not chosen: they are worked out from the network's real size, its shape and the thickness of its cords, using the wave speed that turgor pressure gives (about 22 metres per second). As the network grows its notes fall. Real hyphae are far too damped to ring, so the ring time is a choice; turning it right down is closest to the truth. Making the network smaller raises every note.
Body drone
A low hum that swells with the flow through the network and brightens with the number of growing tips.

What is real and what is not

The timing is the model's: when things branch, fuse, fruit and spike. The sounds are choices. The electrical rhythm comes from measurements that are themselves disputed, and the model's wave shape is not a recorded one. The recording keeps everything unscaled, with two clocks: the time the network grew for, and the much shorter time its electrical activity was listened to at each stage. They are sped up by different amounts, both shown. More in docs/SOUND.md.
